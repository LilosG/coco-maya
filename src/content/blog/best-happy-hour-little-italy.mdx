Happy hour in Little Italy is a competitive field. Most spots run some version of a discounted afternoon window, and the differences between them come down to three things: the quality of the drinks, the setting you're sitting in, and whether the program feels like it was designed with any intention. Coco Maya's [happy hour](/happy-hour) — Monday through Friday, 4:00–6:00 PM on the rooftop patio — holds up on all three.

## The Hours

Happy hour runs **Monday through Friday, 4:00–6:00 PM**. Walk-ins are welcome during the window, though the patio fills up on Thursday and Friday afternoons, especially in the 5–6 PM stretch. If you're bringing more than two or three people, a reservation is worth making ahead of time.

## The Food Menu

Four bites, each built to work alongside a cocktail without being an afterthought:

- **Meatball Sub** — Two meatballs, frankfurter roll, sauce, melted mozzarella
- **HH Pizza: Chefs Wim Pizza** — The chef's call on the build, changes based on what's in rotation
- **Girls Dinner** — Truffle fries and caesar
- **Fried Chicken** — Two pieces, compressed watermelon

The fried chicken is probably the most-ordered item at the moment. The Girls Dinner is a solid pairing with any of the lighter cocktails.

## The Drink Menu

Four cocktails developed for the happy hour window specifically — not just the regular menu repriced:

- **Oroma Espresso Martini or Carajillo** — Two versions of the espresso cocktail depending on what you're after
- **Main Character Energy** — Vodka, pear, orgeat, orange blossom water (an army-navy tiki riff)
- **Out of Office** — Bourbon, peach, lemon, falernum, ginger beer (a Kentucky mule riff)
- **Gin-Othy** — Gin, pear cognac, spiced apple cordial, caramelized pineapple, lemon (a Washington apple riff)

The Out of Office and Main Character Energy are the two that move the most. If you're an espresso martini person, the Carajillo version of the Oroma is the slightly more complex route.

## The Setting

Coco Maya's glass atrium patio is an elevated open-air bar with views over Little Italy. It's one of the more distinctive spots in the neighborhood for an afternoon drink — not a rooftop deck in the conventional sense, but an enclosed-yet-open glass structure that sits above street level. The late afternoon light through the glass is worth timing your visit around.

## The Bottom Line

If you're looking for happy hour in Little Italy, this is a strong option from 4–6 PM on weekdays. Four focused bites, four dedicated cocktails, and a patio setting that doesn't feel like a consolation prize. Walk-in friendly, reservation-smart if you're coming with a group.
