## Breaking Down the Per-Person Food Cost

The foundation of your private dinner budget is the menu. At Coco Maya, food pricing for private events typically ranges from $35 to $65 per person, depending on what you choose and how you structure the meal.

**Entry-level menu ($35–$45 per person):** Think two passed appetizers, a simple salad or soup course, and a choice of two entrées. You might offer a fish option and a meat option, with a basic vegetarian plate available. This approach works well for corporate mixers or casual celebrations where people want to eat but aren't expecting a tasting menu experience.

**Mid-tier menu ($50–$60 per person):** This is where most groups land. You get three to four passed appetizers, a more developed starter course, and three entrée options with better protein quality. Coco Maya's coastal American cuisine means you're looking at things like fresh fish preparations, grass-fed beef, or seasonal vegetables done right. This tier includes a dessert course—not elaborate, but thoughtful.

**Premium menu ($65+ per person):** A full multi-course experience with chef-curated dishes, premium proteins, and refined plating. This includes amuse-bouches, a composed first course, a palate cleanser, and a dessert that feels special. You're paying for technique and ingredient quality here.

For 30 people, the math is straightforward: a $50-per-person menu means $1,500 in food costs before drinks, service, or taxes.

## Beverage Packages and How They Add Up

Drinks are where private dinner budgets either stay reasonable or climb fast. You have three main approaches:

**No hosted bar:** Guests pay for their own drinks. You're looking at minimal cost to you, but it feels cheap and creates friction at the door. Not recommended unless you're explicitly asking people to buy their own.

**Limited hosted bar ($8–$12 per person):** Beer, wine, and one or two signature cocktails. Coco Maya can build a signature drink tied to your event's theme—something that feels personal without requiring the bartender to make 15 different cocktails. For 30 people, budget $240–$360 for the evening. This works if your crowd isn't heavy drinkers or if the dinner is earlier in the day.

**Full open bar ($15–$25 per person):** Premium spirits, full wine list, craft cocktails, non-alcoholic options. Most groups drinking for three to four hours land here. At $20 per person, that's $600 for 30 people. Coco Maya's bar program leans into coastal cocktails and California wines, so you're not paying for premium pricing on basic well liquor—you're getting quality.

**Wine service ($10–$18 per person):** If you want to skip the full bar, a wine-focused approach works well with Modern American coastal cuisine. You might offer a white, a red, and a rosé, plus beer. This feels intentional and keeps costs moderate.

The honest take: full open bar is the safest bet for a private dinner. People expect it, the bartender can manage flow, and you avoid the awkwardness of explaining what's included.

## Service Charges and Taxes

This is where transparency matters. Coco Maya charges a 20% service charge on food and beverages. For a $1,500 food bill plus $600 in drinks, that's $420 in service charges. That goes to your server, bartender, and kitchen support—real people doing the work.

Then add tax. San Diego's sales tax is 7.75%. On your $2,100 subtotal (food plus drinks), that's $162.75.

So your real cost for food, drinks, service, and tax on a mid-tier menu for 30 people:
- Food: $1,500
- Drinks: $600
- Service (20%): $420
- Tax (7.75%): $162.75

## Additional Costs and Considerations

Beyond food, drinks, and service, a few other line items can show up:

**Patio access and setup:** Coco Maya's patio is part of your private event space. Basic setup—tables, chairs, linens—is included. If you want specialty décor, uplighting, or a DJ, those are add-ons and will run $200–$500 depending on scope.

**Audio/visual:** If you're doing toasts, a slideshow, or background music beyond what the restaurant provides, AV rental through a third party is available. Budget $300–$600 for basic projector and sound.

**Staffing upgrades:** The standard package includes one server and one bartender for groups up to 30. If you want dedicated coat check or additional bar support, that's roughly $25–$35 per hour per person.

**Gratuity on top of service charge:** The 20% service charge is distributed among staff. If you want to add additional gratuity (common for exceptional service), that's your choice and typically 5–10% of the bill.

## How to Stretch Your Budget Without Cutting Quality

**Limit the menu choices.** Instead of three entrée options, offer two. This reduces kitchen complexity, speeds service, and feels more intentional. People don't mind fewer choices if the options are good.

**Go wine-focused instead of full bar.** You save $5–$10 per person and the service feels curated. Coco Maya's wine list tilts toward California producers, which pairs naturally with the food.

**Daytime or early evening slots.** Private dinners at 5 or 6 p.m. often have lower minimums or more flexibility than 8 p.m. slots. You're also working with natural light on the patio, which reduces lighting needs.

**Passed appetizers instead of a plated first course.** Passed apps feel interactive and celebratory, cost less than a plated course, and keep the energy moving. Coco Maya's kitchen can handle this smoothly.

**Skip the separate dessert course.** Offer dessert as a self-serve station or a single plated option for everyone. You save $3–$5 per person and the meal still feels complete.

**Host your event on a weeknight.** Thursday or Sunday dinners often have more availability and better pricing than Friday or Saturday. In Little Italy, weeknight events still feel special—the neighborhood has its own rhythm.
